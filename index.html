Intelligence may not be centralized. Complex systems coordinate without central authority through local decisions and distributed signaling. The metaphor applies to brains, colonies, networks, and organizations.

Agent security is not about preventing bad agents — it's about preventing agent identity misuse. Current IAM is built for persistent human identity; it's inadequate for ephemeral agent identity. 22.Not Even Wrong

Harry Law | Cosmos Institute high
8/10
Applies physicist Wolfgang Pauli's phrase "not even wrong" to P(doom) estimates. AI extinction risk estimates are unfalsifiable and therefore scientifically meaningless. These aren't probabilities — they're expressions of aesthetic preference or tribal affiliation.
High relevance for separating signal from noise in AI discourse. The article doesn't take a position on whether AI risk is real, only that quantifying it is methodologically flawed. This should sharpen Chris's thinking about which concerns are evidence-based vs. tribal positions.
Key Insights

Examples: Yudkowsky: 95-100%, Hinton: 10-20%, Hutter: ~5%.

Law's argument: These aren't probabilities (which require reference class of similar events and historical frequency data). They're expressions of aesthetic preference or tribal affiliation.

Framework clash: Yudkowsky's frame (AI is qualitatively different, extinction is likely) produces 95%. Hinton's frame (progress faster than control, but alignment is solvable) produces 10%. They're not disagreeing about facts — they're disagreeing about what kind of question this is.

Honest statement: "I'm uncertain about extinction risk because I'm reasoning about something without precedent," not "I've calculated P(doom) = 0.15."

Assigning probabilities to unprecedented events is not analysis — it's betting on your intuitions and calling it science. The appropriate response to genuine uncertainty is intellectual humility, not false precision.
